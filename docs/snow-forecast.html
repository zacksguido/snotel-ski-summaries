Why the elevation columns? The ECMWF model divides the world into boxes about 25 km (15 miles) across and calculates snowfall for each box as a whole, at the box's average terrain height (“Model grid elev.”). In the mountains that average is usually far below the actual slopes, because peaks and valleys are smoothed together. “Grid vs. mid” compares it with the middle of each ski area's vertical (halfway between its base and summit, from published figures). A large negative number means the model is calculating snow for much lower, warmer terrain: it may call precipitation rain when it is snowing on the mountain, and it misses the extra snow that mountains squeeze out of storms, so totals are likely too low.

Forecast snowfall is new snow depth, converted from the model's snow water at a fixed 7:1 ratio (7 inches of snow per inch of water). Cold, dry mountain snow is often 12:1 to 20:1, so totals may understate powder depth. Forecasts beyond about 3 days are uncertain.
